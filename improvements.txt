Summary of Changes to Improve LSTM Model Accuracy
We made several strategic changes to the LSTM model to improve its prediction accuracy for PM2.5 concentrations. Here's a summary of each change, the rationale behind it, and the results:

Change 1: Simplifying Feature Engineering
What: Reduced the number of features by removing redundant and potentially noisy features like polynomial features, interaction terms, and excessive cyclical features.

Why: The model was likely overfitting due to too many features. Simplifying the feature set helps the model generalize better to unseen data.

Result: MAE improved from 41.44 to 27.87, showing that a more focused feature set helped reduce overfitting.

Change 2: Improved Data Normalization
What: Implemented robust scaling for features and log transformation for the target variable (PM2.5).

Why: PM2.5 data is often skewed with outliers. Log transformation helps normalize the distribution, while robust scaling (using median and IQR) is less sensitive to outliers than standard scaling.

Result: MAE further improved to 22.64, demonstrating that better normalization techniques can significantly impact model performance.

Change 3: Advanced Model Architecture with Residual Connections
What: Implemented a stacked LSTM with residual connections, batch normalization, and recurrent dropout.

Why:

Residual connections help mitigate the vanishing gradient problem in deep networks
Batch normalization stabilizes and accelerates training
Recurrent dropout specifically regularizes the recurrent connections in LSTM cells
Result: Dramatic improvement with MAE reduced to 4.84 and RMSE to 7.36, exceeding our target of around 10.

Change 4: Enhanced Training Strategy
What: Implemented a learning rate schedule with warm-up and decay phases, increased patience for early stopping, and added more sophisticated callbacks.

Why:

Warm-up helps avoid initial training instability
Gradual decay helps fine-tune weights as training progresses
Increased patience prevents premature stopping during temporary plateaus
Result: Contributed to the overall improvement in model performance, allowing the model to train more effectively.

Overall Impact
We achieved a remarkable improvement in prediction accuracy:

Initial MAE: 41.44
Final MAE: 4.84 (88% reduction)
Final RMSE: 7.36
These results significantly exceed our target of getting the MAE around 10, demonstrating that a combination of proper data preprocessing, appropriate model architecture, and effective training strategies can dramatically improve LSTM performance for time series forecasting tasks.
